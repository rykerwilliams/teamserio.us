# The 20th anniversary interviews — format

Short "life story" interviews with Team Serious members. Fifteen minutes each,
two or three per episode, so nothing runs past half an hour.

The trap with a life-story interview is that it turns into a CV read aloud.
The trap with an irreverent one is that it turns into a bit, and you come away
knowing nothing about the person. This format alternates: every real question
is followed by a stupid one, and the stupid ones are where people relax enough
to say something true.

**Nobody prepares.** The guest gets the shape, not the questions. One
exception: the artifact, below, which they need a day's warning for.

---

## The fifteen minutes

**0:00 — Cold open (30 seconds).** One absurd question, asked before any
introduction, answered before the guest knows the tape is rolling. No
follow-up. Cut straight to the theme.

> Stadium or ballpark mustard, and how angry are you about the question?

**0:30 — How you got here (3 minutes).** Two questions, no more:
how they found Magic, and who dragged them into Team Serious. Names and a year.
If they start listing finishes, let them, then move on.

**3:30 — The artifact (3 minutes).** They bring **one object** and talk about
it. A card, a trophy, a receipt, a sleeve, a photograph, a scar. This is the
only thing they're told in advance, because the preparation is the point —
whatever they choose already says something. Ask what it is, then ask why that
one, then stop talking.

**6:30 — The incident (4 minutes).** One story from one event. Not their best
finish — the one they *tell*. Prompt with a year or a venue if they stall:

> Something happened at [event] that you still bring up. Go.

Four minutes is roughly one story. If they finish early, ask who else was
there and what that person's version would be.

**10:30 — Rapid fire (3 minutes).** Six questions from the bank in
`_data/bio_questions.yml`, read fast, no follow-ups, no explanations allowed.
Pick ones their written answers were short on — you already have their
answers, so chase the gaps. The rule is theirs to break, and the best moments
are when they break it.

**13:30 — The handoff (90 seconds).** The guest asks a question of the next
guest, without knowing who that is. It gets asked for real at the top of the
next interview, before the cold open. This chains the series together and
gives the team a way to needle each other across episodes.

---

## Running it

- **Two or three guests per episode**, grouped by something: same era, same
  event, same city, same deck, people who dislike each other politely.
- **Record more than you need.** Fifteen minutes is the cut, not the session.
  Twenty-five minutes of tape makes a much better fifteen.
- **Don't fix the stumbles.** The register is a kitchen table, not a studio.
- **The host's job is to stop talking.** Every question above is short on
  purpose. Ask it, then wait longer than is comfortable.
- **Get the names right.** People will mention others constantly; note the
  spellings as you go, for the write-up and for crediting the bios.

## What comes out of it

- An episode for the podcast feed.
- Pull quotes for the person's bio page.
- Names and events that belong in the archive — several of the TMD write-ups
  surfaced people we had no record of, and this will do the same.
